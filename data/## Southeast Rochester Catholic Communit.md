## Have you visited a Repair Café?

A free community event where you bring broken items (clothes, lamps, toys, small electronics). Skilled volunteers (neighbors) help you diagnose and repair them, teaching you the skills as you go. There is no fee for this (volunteer) service, and the benefits are many! It's about extending product life and keeping things out of landfills. Color Fairport Green holds their Repair Café on the 2nd and 4th Thursdays of every month from 5-7pm at the Fairport United Methodist Church. ## DIOCESE OF ROCHESTER PERMANENT DIACONATE

The deacon is a man of faith, who is called from the community that already recognizes his dedication to service. He makes a lifetime commitment to serving the People of God by proclaiming the Word, assisting and presiding at liturgies, and ministering in the areas of charity and social justice. A deacon gives witness to his availability to the community by addressing present and emerging needs of the Church. The Diocese is inviting interested men of strong and active faith, who can demonstrate a record of service in the communities in which they live, work and worship, who may wish to explore a possible call to ordained ministry as a permanent deacon, to first speak with their pastor for an initial conversation. They may also contact Deacon Edward Giblin, Director of the Office of t h e Permanent Diaconate, a t edward.giblin@dor.org , for additional information. A potential candidate must be at least 35 years of age and no older than 62 years of age at the time of ordination after a five (5) year period of formation. He must be in good health, emotionally mature, have stable relationships and must have the wholehearted support of his wife (if married) and family to enter the phase of inquiry. Because the parish is the primary experience of Church for most inquirers, the parish community is asked as well to invite from among its members men who exhibit strong faith and a spirit of service and encourage them to consider a possible vocation to the permanent diaconate.

## A Word from Fr. John...

After consulting with the Finance Councils in each of our three churches, in the spirit of openness, we have inserted a short financial report for the first six months of the fiscal year, July 1, 2025 to December 31, 2026. We believe this was important as our churches are grappling with various financial challenges. While the situation may not be ideal for some, it has not yet reached a crisis level requiring drastic measures. However, the trends indicate that we must take proactive steps to halt and reverse them.

On the positive side, we have some time to address these issues. Our Parish Pastoral Council is working to develop a range of strategies to achieve this. Some of those initial efforts, including 'Pray It Forward' and 'Nametag Sunday,' have been well received. They are hoping to expand those efforts in the coming months.

Turning our attention to the financial report, the area of greatest concern is at St. Boniface. As reported in the Annual Report shared with the parish in September, St. Boniface is continuing to face a significant operating deficit, due to the continuing decline in collections. To address this, we have implemented various cost-cutting measures, including replacing the full-time maintenance person shared with Nativity Prep with a part-time maintenance person who works only for the church. As the report shows, the expenses are running favorable to budget by $11,535.

Additionally, we are in the process of renegotiating our lease with Nativity Prep to secure an increase in lease payments, which would start next fiscal year. We anticipate completing this process soon and kindly request your prayers for a favorable outcome. While these efforts have reduced the operating deficit, we still project a $75,000 deficit for the fiscal year. To cover this deficit, we will draw from our stable patrimony (investments/savings) . These investments generally grow by around 5% annually over time. Therefore, drawing 5% of twenty quarter average usually won't deplete the principal. For St. Boniface, this translates to an annual draw of approximately $50,000. However, this amount does not cover our operating deficit. Consequently, we are forced to deplete our savings to cover repeated significant operating deficits, which is unsustainable for more than a few years.

While we continue to look for ways to reduce expenses and increase revenues, we kindly request the assistance of our parishioners in increasing our collections. We understand that our parishioners are facing challenges similar to the parish, particularly in terms of rising costs, especially for electricity, natural gas, and insurance. If you are able to contribute more to the collection, we would greatly appreciate it. If you are not able to increase your contribution, we kindly request that you actively recruit others to join the parish. By doing so, even if they are only be able to match your level of giving, your efforts would double the amount of your contribution. My sense is that most of our parishioners have deep love for their particular church. Why not share that love by inviting others to join us.

While St. Mary's also faces an operating deficit, their relatively larger stable patrimony allows them to cover their losses with a 5% draw. This is aided in part by relatively stronger collections. However, St. Mary's is currently facing a significant expense for replacing the large air conditioning unit for the church. We anticipate funding this expense by withdrawing funds from St. Mary's stable patrimony.

In contrast, Blessed Sacrament has an operating surplus due to the revenue generated from leasing the school building to Innova Girls Academy, a charter school. However, starting this fall, Innova will occupy the entire school, necessitating renovations to the lower level of the church to accommodate Sunday morning faith formation classes. Furthermore, the Financial Forecast Model shared by the Parish Pastoral Council last fall projects that if current trends persist, that within the next 5+ years, St. Mary's along with Blessed Sacrament may face similar financial challenges as St. Boniface currently does.

Again, we are sharing this information with you, because we still have time to turn things around. However, that will require collective effort from all of us. The recent initiatives of Parish Pastoral Council are a positive step, and more will follow. I have faith in you and our ability to overcome these challenges. I am also hopeful, perhaps even optimistic, that the arrival of a new bishop will provide an opportunity to renew and revitalize the Church in our diocese.

Please join me in praying for God's blessings on our parish churches, Bishop Bonnici, and our diocese as we navigate these challenges and seize the opportunities that arise. Thank you.

Peace and blessings.

Yours, Fr. John
